this one's a bit of a vent post, apologies.

---

lately i've been sitting on a frustration that has long been a thorn in my side, but only recently has it become illumminated to me how bad it truly is. like a lot transwomen, my socialization is largely done online. i hang out with a group of like-minded trans and enby weirdos in a little server i made, we play games, we joke about kissing each other while calling each other sister, y'know, the usual. i really appreciate these online friends i've made (some of whom i've met in person, even!), but staying inside and playing on the computer can be a bit lonely, so i also have a bunch of offline cis friends that are largely a holdover from when i was in high school, and some adjacent individuals who were dragged in over time by mutual friends.

now, it'd be in bad faith to go and describe these people as bigoted or transphobic. there are times we don't align politically, but largely the feeling is that everyone there is cool with me, my wife and a couple of others being queer and / or trans. they do their best to be respectful, and there's times corrections need to be made but it's not with malice. for years i lived my life comfortable in this experience, and didn't really question it.

but, my life isn't static. i'm trans, and i want to meet more people locally (and remotely) that are trans as well. it's a little selfish, and not every trans person is going to become my bestie. i'm aware that people are more than their identity, and certainly i wouldn't want someone boiling me down to just that. it just helps knowing people who are *like* you, right? so, in the past few years, i've made that effort. i've been to seattle a few times, met a lot of cool people, either in person for the first time or just outright for the first time. i reconnected with a transgirl i knew recently and got to know her a bit more, and we bonded over the experience of being perverted weirdos on the online.

these meetups are fun, but inevitably i am forced to return to the same old. it wouldn't be so bad, but these brief visits where i'm able to go fully mask off really do illuminate how bad it feels when someone casually does something unknowingly to step on your toes. it's little things but i still get the occasional misgendering. my family still calls me my dead name sometimes. generally, people aren't as cognizant of the issues you deal with, and that just comes from the territory of being a person. i'm not that hung up on that.

none of this really became a huge issue in my mind until hogwarts legacy came out, and at least one friend felt that it was far more important that he play it than pay any mind to the discourse surrounding it.

i tried so hard at the time to explain to them the ways that their money doesn't just indirectly fund anti-trans legislation, it is something explicitly stated by rowling to be an aspirational goal. it is something she has vowed to fight, and every dollar going for her goes back into this insane crusade against less than 1% of the global population. i explained i know people in the UK who are likely to fall victim to this legislation, and i asked them to reconsider blowing $100 CAD on some worthless AAA game, and at least consider pirating it.

i wasn't just ignored, but i was treated as irrational. i was told that all consumption under capitalism is unethical, that i was overreacting, that it was enough to just be an "ally" and "not endorse her views" and that i should just stop being such a stick in the mud. i really resent that i didn't say more in response. i chose to shut up, because it became blatantly apparent that i was the enemy in the room. all of my friends, such staunch allies before, suddenly felt that it was ok to talk down to a transwoman asking to just this once consider not funding the genocide of people like her.

at the time this stung and i let it pass -- but every time i talk about it now, my online friends react in horror. i don't really have a good response to it. i tell them i prefer to be nonconfrontational, and recognize people have differing views on this stuff. inevitably the question cycles back to "why are you still friends with these people?". i'd like to think my relationship is predicated on more than political disagreements, but it's hard to ignore the impacts of rowling's hate. her money has [directly contributed to the erasure of transwomen legally in the UK](https://www.thepinknews.com/2024/02/19/jk-rowling-for-women-scotland-donation-legal-definition-woman/). i *know* multiple people personally affected by that. aren't i doing a disservice to my british friends for not standing up for *them*?

there's been a variety of other incidents, but largely the felt feeling is that the things i like are weird and gross, and the things i believe in are pointless virtue signalling. i've been told by at least one friend that he "doesn't believe" that transness is the correct way forward for people experiencing gender dysphoria. i tolerate someone in our hangout discord who i vehemently dislike for being an edgy chud who posts weird nazi-adjacent bullshit and line-treading centrism crap, all because my other friends really like him. the cognitive dissonance has become pretty hard to ignore between the handful of trans people who continue to exist in that space, and we often retreat from it when the noise becomes too much.

i have all the power in the world as the administrator of this discord to tell these people off, to ensure this behaviour stops, but it all comes at the cost of my social standing. i'd just make an enemy of myself, just like before. so i sit here, constantly feeling disrespected and ignored, because i dunno if i have another choice.

hopefully i can meet more trans people locally.
